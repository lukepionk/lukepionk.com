# Personal website v2

## Purpose

The website should earn its place beside LinkedIn. It should reveal Luke’s
judgment, range, working style, and taste in ways a standardized profile cannot,
while still giving a new contact public evidence and a direct contact path.

The page keeps five sections: Header, Now, Work, Resources, Contact. It remains
static HTML and CSS with no runtime JavaScript, remote fonts, tracking, cookies,
database, or application server.

## Identity

Luke’s background crosses audit, finance, operations, analytics, security, and
software. The broader throughline is understanding the whole problem, earning
trust across different kinds of people, building for real workflows, taking
responsibility beyond the prototype, and committing to useful work for the long
term.

The public copy summarizes that range without copying private documents,
employer details, project names, metrics, titles, or claims that require
reconciliation. Engineering remains important evidence, but it no longer
defines the whole identity.

## Visual direction

The direction combines three reference strengths without copying their brands:

- [Squarespace’s personal website examples](https://ja.squarespace.com/blog/personal-website-examples)
  use strong visual identity and emphasize showing who a person is and what
  they care about.
- [Apple](https://www.apple.com/) gives each large module one dominant message,
  uses strong contrast between chapters, and keeps actions short and obvious.
- [AWS Free Tier](https://aws.amazon.com/free/) uses outcome-led headlines,
  modular cards, clear calls to action, and structured supporting proof.

The resulting style is warm modernism: contemporary system typography,
generous space, rounded modules, ivory and dark green foundations, a clay
accent, and a simple coffee mug mark. The mug is a personal signature and
favicon, not a coffee-themed brand.

## Page structure

1. **Header and hero:** name, location, a broad thesis, two actions, a concise
   career path, and an original coffee-centered illustration.
2. **Now:** four cards that explain Luke’s approach—start with the work, earn
   trust, use the whole range, and stay accountable.
3. **Work:** one public case study, framed around purpose and decisions before
   tools, with a visual release path as supporting proof.
4. **Resources:** five compact cards with one useful idea from each source.
5. **Contact:** a high-contrast closing chapter with email, LinkedIn, and GitHub.

## Content boundaries

- Do not publish private employer material, internal names, metrics, résumé
  source text, cover-letter text, or unverified claims.
- Do not invent a second project, testimonial, endorsement, or reading history.
- Do not add a stock photo or generic technology image. Use an approved personal
  photograph later if it adds real identity.
- Keep the project’s technical proof, but make it support the human and business
  story rather than dominate it.

## Review questions

For the next human review, ask:

1. What kind of person does Luke seem to be?
2. What range of problems does he appear equipped to handle?
3. What principle or phrase is memorable?
4. What public evidence did you find?
5. How would you contact him?

The design succeeds when a visitor sees a multidimensional problem solver with
technical depth, business context, and a human approach—not merely a software
portfolio.
