# Website v3: content first

Status: visual revision prepared locally September 30, 2026. Supersedes the
visual direction in [v2](v2.md); the five-section structure is unchanged.

## Why

Luke wants a restrained, professional page that makes substantive work easy
to recognize. The first text-only version made projects blend into the rest
of the page. His reference was Zach Pelka's project portfolio: distinct project
identities, a clear hierarchy, and an easy way to scan scope and results.
Kanbrick's use of navy, gold, and alternating light and dark sections informs
the color palette.

## Decisions

- A warm ivory page, navy type, and gold accents. The navy header, featured
  work, and Contact panel establish contrast across the page. Gold highlights
  actions and key labels; supporting work uses pale blue and sand.
- Use navy text on gold buttons and gold text on navy panels. Keep small text
  on light backgrounds dark enough to read comfortably.
- A 68rem desktop frame, with shorter text measures inside it. Two-column
  content stacks into one column on phones.
- Larger hero and section headings establish hierarchy. Space, borders, and
  background color group related content.
- Hero: role and city, what Luke builds, ownership from understanding the
  workflow through code and release, background, and email, LinkedIn, and GitHub.
- Name Amazon as Luke's current employer and Amazon Web Services, Deloitte,
  and PwC as previous employers. Use plain text, with no implied endorsement.
- Show CISSP, CPA, Claude Certified Architect — Foundations, and the Carnegie
  Mellon Privacy Engineering certificate near the hero. Distinguish a
  university certificate from a degree. Describe MLU Champion recognition and
  peer mentoring in Learning rather than presenting them as certifications.
- A full-width navy feature panel gives the AI work priority. Reporting and
  financial validation sit in two supporting cards. Each card identifies the
  area of work, leads with an outcome, and separates Luke's contribution.
- Original inline category icons aid scanning. They are decorative, with no
  product screenshots, dashboards, or implied performance data.
- Lessons use a two-column grid. Learning pairs an introduction with its
  existing resource selector, and Contact has a distinct closing panel.
- Work comes before the lessons. Each example leads with what changed for
  users, then explains Luke's contribution. The website itself is not a
  featured work example.
- No headshot for now. If one is added later, it belongs near Contact, and the
  publish allowlist and tests must change deliberately.

## Checks

Browser tests cover 320, 390, 720, 768, 1024, and desktop widths for overflow,
accessibility at phone and tablet sizes, and 44px tap targets for the hero and
contact links.

## Learning

The Resources section is now "Learning," with a Read / Listen / Watch toggle
built from radio buttons and CSS (no JavaScript). Only sources Luke has
confirmed he uses belong here; books and films wait until he names them.

## Presentation refinement

Make hands-on agent development and delivery clear at the top of the page.
Keep the lessons specific: human sign-off belongs to the compliance-review
example, and prototypes help people evaluate a proposal. The navigation calls
these lessons "Approach"; the existing `#now` anchor remains valid.

## Work and outcomes

Feature delivered professional work across AI, analytics, and operational
finance. Keep results distinct from implementation details: a technology
choice, release, or large codebase does not establish a useful outcome.
Describe the benefit and the work Luke owned, with enough context to assess
the claim. Keep pilot results scoped to the pilot and distinguish measured
benefits from projected savings. Use specific qualitative outcomes when a
number is not confirmed for publication.

Emphasize production AI assistants, tool integration, evaluation, release
ownership, and collaboration in the featured case. The reporting case shows
both hands-on data engineering and ownership of a reporting roadmap; the
finance case shows how Luke assesses claimed value.

Public summaries omit internal operating metrics and launch dates. Describing
an outcome accurately does not establish that its underlying business data is
cleared for disclosure. Keep source documents and detailed evidence private,
including in the Git history of any future public branch.

## Writing (planned)

Luke wants a Writing page as a scratch pad for learning notes. He will send
rough notes and the agent will shape each into a post for his review. The page
and nav link ship with the first real post, not before. Adding it requires:
allowing `writing/` pages in `scripts/publish.py` (today it accepts exactly six
top-level files), serving `/writing/` paths from CloudFront, and adding the
pages to the sitemap and tests. Every post follows the same content rules as
the site: public professional affiliations are appropriate; employer internals
and operating metrics stay private.
