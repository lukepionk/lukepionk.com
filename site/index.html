Now: AI and data systems · Before: analytics, finance, IT audit and security

Lessons I build by.

Each one came from real work, and most came from getting something wrong first.

Decide what has to be trusted first.

My first attempt at automating a compliance review asked a model to do the whole job. The demos looked impressive, but the answers changed between runs. Rules now carry that load, and the model handles only the judgment calls.

Show a working version.

A written proposal to explore automation didn’t win support. A small prototype, tested with the people who would use it, did. Now I build something real before I argue for it.

Write down what the experts know.

The most useful automated checks I’ve built came from sitting with specialists and recording what they actually look for. It also made them co-authors, and co-authors champion the result.

Check the claimed value.

In finance, I tested whether a promised improvement showed up in actual performance before counting it as savings. I hold software to the same standard: measure whether it helped.
