Lessons I build by.

Each one came from real work, and most came from getting something wrong first.

Decide what has to be trusted first.

My first attempt at automating a compliance review asked a model to do the whole job. The demos looked impressive, but the answers changed between runs. Rules now carry that load, and the model handles only the judgment calls.

Show a working version.

A written proposal to explore automation didn’t win support. A small prototype, tested with the people who would use it, did. Now I build something real before I argue for it.

Write down what the experts know.

The most useful automated checks I’ve built came from sitting with specialists and recording what they actually look for. It also made them co-authors, and co-authors champion the result.

Check the claimed value.

In finance, I tested whether a promised improvement showed up in actual performance before counting it as savings. I hold software to the same standard: measure whether it helped.

Resources

Where I actually learn: the model makers’ own documentation, a few great technical teachers, and the business side of my CPA brain.

  • Model system cards Anthropic
    Before building on a new model, read what its makers tested, where it fell short, and what they chose not to claim. OpenAI’s system cards serve the same purpose.
  • Building Effective Agents Anthropic
    Its practical bias is restraint: start with simple workflows and add autonomy only when the task warrants it.
  • Andrej Karpathy YouTube
    From-scratch lectures on how language models are actually built, for when an abstraction stops being enough.
  • 3Blue1Brown YouTube
    Visual explanations of neural networks and transformers that make the math feel inspectable.
  • Aswath Damodaran YouTube
    An NYU finance professor on valuing a business: tell its story, then test the story with numbers.
  • Acquired Podcast
    Long histories of how great companies were built, useful for thinking about what makes a business durable.
  • Do Things That Don’t Scale Paul Graham
    Its case is to stay close enough to early users that their awkward workarounds reshape what you build.
